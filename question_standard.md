# Question Generation Standard

## The Unified Principle of Question Engineering

### 1) Multi-Layered Processing
Engineer a "gate" where the candidate must perform at least two cognitive leaps before selecting an answer:
- **The What**: Identify the hidden concept.
- **The How**: Apply a specific rule or formula.

### 2) Plausible Pathological Distractors
Design wrong answers that are mathematically or logically "correct" **if** a candidate makes exactly one common professional error (e.g., forgetting a unit conversion, ignoring an ethical constraint, or failing to prioritize).

### 3) The "Noise-to-Signal" Ratio
Deliberately embed red herrings or extraneous data to test the candidate’s ability to filter irrelevant information, simulating real-world professional chaos.

### 4) Cognitive Load Manipulation
Utilize formats that increase mental effort to ensure only those with true fluency (deeply internalized knowledge) can finish within the time limit, such as:
- **Negative stems**: EXCEPT / NOT
- **SATA**: Select-all-that-apply

### 5) The "First-Principle" Requirement
Construct the stem (the question) so it is independent of the choices, meaning the candidate must generate the correct solution mentally rather than recognizing it through elimination.

### 6) Safety/Priority Hierarchies
In high-stakes fields (Medicine/Law/Engineering), engineer questions where all options are good, but only one is essential, first, or legally defensible under the specific constraints provided.
